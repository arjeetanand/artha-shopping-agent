3. PROJECT DESCRIPTION — COPY THIS PARAGRAPH ONLY

Artha is an AI shopping research assistant that turns a natural-language brief,
reference image, or product link into a source-linked purchase plan. Instead of
opening dozens of tabs, shoppers describe their budget, preferences, location,
and delivery needs. Artha clarifies missing details, searches retailer sources,
compares products, and ranks options against those constraints. Each recommendation
keeps supporting links and highlights unknown prices, stock, variants, or delivery
information rather than inventing certainty. A conversational interface supports
typed and voice interactions, while signed-in users can save private purchase
plans. Built with React, a Python backend, SQLite, and configurable AI, search,
and speech providers, the prototype explores a more transparent way to shop.
Real purchases happen on retailer websites; the local order flow is explicitly
labeled as a sandbox.

4. PROBLEM / SOLUTION / DIFFERENTIATOR — OPTIONAL FORM ANSWERS

Problem:
Online shopping requires juggling retailer tabs, checking inconsistent product
information, and manually comparing price, fit, and delivery against a budget.

Solution:
Artha captures the shopper's constraints, researches retailer sources, and creates
an explainable shortlist with a purchase plan and links to supporting evidence.

What makes it different:
Artha exposes uncertainty. Missing or conflicting evidence stays visible, and
recommendations distinguish a checked fit from an option requiring verification.
It supports follow-up refinement without making shoppers repeat the whole brief.

Technology:
React + Vite frontend; Python backend; SQLite; Anakin retailer search; configurable
Gemini or Ollama brief extraction; configurable speech output, including SLNG.
Mention only the providers actually shown in your recording.

Prototype limits:
Retailer checkout happens outside Artha. Order tracking in the local prototype
is a labeled sandbox. Delivery, stock, and final prices need retailer confirmation.

I built Artha, an AI shopping research assistant that turns a shopping brief
into a source-linked purchase plan.

The problem I wanted to solve: finding a product is easy; comparing budget,
preferences, delivery, and incomplete retailer information takes far more work.

With Artha, a shopper can describe what they need, inspect a ranked shortlist,
check the supporting sources, and refine the request through conversation.
Missing evidence stays visible, so uncertainty is part of the answer.

Building the prototype taught me that useful AI needs more than a fluent reply:
it needs clear constraints, inspectable evidence, and honest limits.
